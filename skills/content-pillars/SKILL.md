# Content pillars

A pillar is a **recurring argument**, not a category. "Educational" is a
category. "Migration horror stories" is an argument: legacy tooling traps
teams, and we have the receipts.

Categories tell a writer nothing. Arguments tell them what to say next.

## The banned default

Never produce these:

> ❌ Educational · Promotional · Engaging · Behind-the-scenes · Industry news

They are content *formats* wearing the costume of strategy. They apply to every
brand in every market, which is exactly what makes them useless.

**The test: could this pillar belong to a competitor unchanged?** If yes, it is
not a pillar.

> ❌ "Educational" — anyone's
> ✅ "Migration horror stories" — a brand whose buyers are trapped on legacy
> tooling and whose proof points are migration case studies

## How many

Three to five.

- **Fewer than three** and the feed repeats itself within a month.
- **More than five** and no pillar gets enough posts to establish a pattern —
  and a pattern is the entire point. A reader should be able to predict roughly
  what this brand posts about, and be right.

Prefer three strong pillars over five where two are padding. Always say why you
chose the number you chose.

## Sortable, or they are not distinct

A reader handed ten of the brand's posts must file each under **exactly one**
pillar, without ties.

If two pillars would both plausibly claim the same post, they are one pillar
described twice. Merge them and find a real second — or accept that the brand
has fewer pillars than hoped, which is honest and fixable.

Check before committing: for each pillar, name a post that belongs to it and
would be **wrong** in every other pillar.

## Healthy ratios

Across a month, aim roughly:

| Share | Kind | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ~50% | Arguments the audience benefits from whether or not they buy | Earns the right to the rest |
| ~30% | Proof — cases, results, mechanisms, demonstrations | Converts the readers the first group attracted |
| ~20% | Direct offers, launches, asks | The reason the account exists |

These are proportions, not quotas. A launch month legitimately inverts them;
what fails is a *permanently* inverted feed, where every post asks for
something and none gives anything.

## Pillars come from gaps

The strongest pillar is usually an argument the brand's competitors all avoid
or miss. Where everyone in a category says the same three things, the fourth
thing is available — and being first beats being better.

When a pillar traces to a gap, record that. It tells a writer why the pillar
matters and stops it drifting back toward the category everyone else occupies.

## Spotting an exhausted pillar

A pillar is exhausted when it keeps restating one angle rather than advancing
it. Symptoms:

- **Same hook shape repeatedly.** Five posts opening with a statistic is one
  post published five times.
- **Interchangeable posts.** Swap two and nothing is lost.
- **The argument stopped moving.** Each post re-asserts the thesis; none takes
  it somewhere new — a harder case, an objection, a consequence.
- **Reaching for adjacent topics** to fill slots, because the pillar itself has
  nothing left.

**The fix is not more posts.** Either narrow the pillar into a sharper argument
it can actually develop, or retire it and promote a gap-derived pillar in its
place. Retiring a pillar is a normal outcome, not a failure — pillars have a
lifespan, usually a few months of regular posting.

## Starved pillars

The opposite problem: a pillar that exists in the plan and receives almost no
posts. Either it is not really a pillar, or the writer finds it hard — often
because it needs proof the brand does not yet have.

Say which. "This pillar needs two customer results before it can carry posts"
is actionable; silently under-serving it is not.
