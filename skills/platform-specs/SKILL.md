# Platform specifications

Drafting guidance for the platforms plgn schedules to. Use it to write posts
that fit before they reach the server.

## The table

| Platform | Hard cap | Target length | Hook style | Hashtags | Links |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | 3,000 | 900–1,300 | First 2 lines before "see more" — a claim or a specific number | 3–5, at the end | Suppressed in-body; put in first comment or accept lower reach |
| **X** | 280 | 180–240 | Whole post is the hook; front-load the surprise | 0–2, inline only if natural | Consume ~23 chars regardless of length |
| **Instagram** | 2,200 | 300–600 | First line only; the rest is truncated behind "more" | 5–10, at the end or first comment | Not clickable in captions — say "link in bio" or omit |
| **Facebook** | 63,206 | 100–250 | Short beats long; the platform buries walls of text | 0–3 | Clickable, with a preview card |
| **TikTok** | 2,200 | 80–150 | Caption supports the video, never replaces it | 3–5, inline | Not clickable |

**Target length, not the cap.** Draft to the target so the server's gate has
headroom. A post written at 2,995 of LinkedIn's 3,000 characters fails the
moment anyone edits a word.

## Platform-native, not cross-posted

The same idea must be **re-expressed** per platform, never pasted. A LinkedIn
post pasted into X is a truncated LinkedIn post, and readers can tell.

Worked example — one idea, three platforms:

> **The idea:** teams waste the first week of every quarter deciding what to
> work on, because last quarter's review never happened.

**LinkedIn** — argument with evidence, room to develop:

> Most teams lose the first week of every quarter.
>
> Not to holidays. To deciding what to do.
>
> The pattern is always the same: the quarter ends, nobody runs the review,
> and the next planning meeting starts from memory instead of data. So the
> first week goes to reconstructing what happened — badly.
>
> The fix costs 90 minutes. Run the review *before* the quarter closes, while
> the context is still in people's heads. Write down what shipped, what
> slipped, and why. Then planning starts from a document instead of an
> argument.
>
> The teams that do this don't plan better. They just start sooner.

**X** — one turn, compressed to the surprise:

> Most teams lose week 1 of every quarter to deciding what to work on.
>
> Not because planning is hard. Because nobody ran last quarter's review, so
> planning starts from memory.
>
> 90 minutes before the quarter closes buys back a week.

**Instagram** — first line carries everything, body is scannable:

> You didn't lose week one to holidays.
>
> You lost it to reconstructing a quarter nobody wrote down.
>
> Run the review before the quarter ends — while people still remember why
> things slipped. 90 minutes, and planning starts from a document instead of
> an argument.
>
> Save this for the last week of the quarter 📌

Note what changed: not just length. The argument's *shape* changed — LinkedIn
gets the mechanism, X gets the punchline, Instagram gets the reframe and a
save prompt. Same idea, three native expressions.

## Hooks that work, by shape

- **The specific number.** "90 minutes" beats "a short meeting."
- **The correction.** Name the assumed cause, then the real one.
- **The cost.** State what the reader is already losing, then the fix.

Avoid: rhetorical questions as openers ("Ever wonder why...?"), "In today's
fast-paced world", and any opener that could preface any post on any topic.

## The server is authoritative

These caps are drafting guidance. plgn's validation gate enforces the real
limits server-side, and the brand's own configuration may be stricter than
this table. **Where they disagree, the server wins** — follow the
**gate-recovery** skill rather than arguing with the error.
