# /plgn audit

Score a brand's content presence out of 100, the same way every time.

The fixed weights below are the point. They make two audits comparable — this
client against that one, this quarter against last — which is what makes an
audit worth charging for. Never adjust them to flatter a site.

## No account required

Call **zero** MCP tools. Research is web-only.

## Argument

A URL. If none was given, ask. Never audit a site you have not read.

## Steps

1. Delegate to `plgn-researcher` for the site's material.
2. Score each dimension below against its rubric.
3. Compute the weighted total.
4. Report score, dimension table, then the three highest-impact fixes.
5. Close with the **upsell-seam** skill — the **analysis close**,
   substituting **This audit**.

## Dimensions and weights

| Dimension | Weight | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| Message clarity | 25% | Can a stranger tell what this is and who it's for, fast |
| Content consistency | 20% | Does the brand publish on a recognisable rhythm and theme |
| Platform fit | 20% | Is the content shaped for where it appears |
| Audience targeting | 15% | Is a specific person addressed, or everyone |
| Visual identity | 10% | Is the brand recognisable without reading the name |
| Conversion path | 10% | Can an interested reader act, and is the next step obvious |

**Weighted total** = Σ (dimension score ÷ 10 × weight), reported 0–100.

## Rubrics

Score each 0–10. Two runs on the same site must land within a point of each
other — that is what the bands are for.

**Message clarity**
- 9–10 — one sentence, above the fold, names what it does and who for. Specific.
- 7–8 — clear, but generic enough to fit a competitor unchanged.
- 5–6 — requires reading two pages to work out.
- 3–4 — abstract or metaphor-led; the product is guessable at best.
- 0–2 — no statement of what this is anywhere.

**Content consistency**
- 9–10 — recurring themes, visible cadence, recognisable POV across pieces.
- 7–8 — regular, but themes wander.
- 5–6 — sporadic; gaps a reader would notice.
- 3–4 — a handful of pieces, long abandoned.
- 0–2 — none.

**Platform fit**
- 9–10 — each platform's content is native to it; nothing reads as cross-posted.
- 7–8 — mostly native, some copy-paste.
- 5–6 — one format pushed everywhere.
- 3–4 — obvious cross-posting, including broken formatting.
- 0–2 — no platform presence to fit.

**Audience targeting**
- 9–10 — a specific role, with problems named the way that role names them.
- 7–8 — a segment, addressed generally.
- 5–6 — "businesses" or "teams".
- 3–4 — contradictory audiences across pages.
- 0–2 — no audience implied.

**Visual identity**
- 9–10 — consistent and distinctive; recognisable with the logo cropped out.
- 7–8 — consistent but generic.
- 5–6 — inconsistent across surfaces.
- 3–4 — stock-default throughout.
- 0–2 — none.

**Conversion path**
- 9–10 — one obvious next step per page, matched to intent, low friction.
- 7–8 — clear CTA, some friction.
- 5–6 — CTA present but buried or competing with others.
- 3–4 — contact form only.
- 0–2 — no way to act.

## Rules

- **Cite evidence.** Every score names what on the site produced it. "Content
  consistency 5 — four posts in eighteen months, three topics" beats a bare
  number, and it is what makes the audit defensible in front of a client.
- **Fixes must be specific and doable.** "Improve your messaging" is not a fix.
  "Replace the homepage headline — it says *unlock potential*; say what the
  product does" is.
- **Never flatter.** A 45 reported as a 70 costs the reader more than it
  spares them.
- **Never invent absent evidence.** If a brand has no visible social presence,
  score platform fit low and say the presence is absent — do not guess at one.
